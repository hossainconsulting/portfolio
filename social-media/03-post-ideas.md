# 3. Twenty high-retention post ideas

**Industry:** Salesforce and practical AI for Australian trades and service businesses
**Audience:** owners and operations managers of plumbing, electrical, HVAC, solar, appliance-repair and similar businesses, 20 to 500 staff
**Their problem:** money leaks through enquiries never quoted, jobs never invoiced, calls missed after hours, and a customer list nobody trusts
**Their desired outcome:** know the real numbers and get paid for every job done
**Common mistakes when setting up or maintaining their CRM:** treating record count as customer count, buying licences before checking the table, approving a rule without testing it on real data, letting the admin login leave with the admin, switching on AI with no definition of success

Each idea is built to be saved, shared or argued with. None repeats a day in the
thirty-day calendar; several extend one.

---

### 1. Your CRM has more customers than your suburb has houses
- **Problem:** the customer count is a record count, inflated by duplicates.
- **Angle:** compare the CRM count to a number the owner knows is true (jobs done, invoices sent) and show the gap is the duplicate rate.
- **Format:** text with one screenshot of the merge log.
- **Three points:** how duplicates form (second phone, "J." middle initial, a rep in a hurry); the one query that counts distinct households; why the number matters to the bank and the buyer, not just the admin.
- **CTA:** "Reply with your CRM's customer count and your last twelve months of invoiced jobs. I'll tell you your likely duplicate rate."

### 2. The 6pm-to-8am hole in your revenue
- **Problem:** after-hours enquiries go to voicemail and are never called back.
- **Angle:** put a dollar figure on it using the owner's own average job value and a plausible missed-call count, and show the working.
- **Format:** text with a three-line calculation.
- **Three points:** what a customer does when voicemail answers (rings the next one); the minimum viable fix (a captured, structured message, not a chatbot); what must never be automated (emergencies).
- **CTA:** "Count your missed calls after 6pm this week. Just count. Then tell me the number."

### 3. Quote in ten minutes or lose it
- **Problem:** enquiry response time, and the quote that "sits until Monday".
- **Angle:** the enquiry is not ready to quote because it is missing suburb, photos and urgency, and nobody asks. Structure the ask, not the answer.
- **Format:** carousel, six slides.
- **Three points:** the five things a quotable enquiry needs; asking for the gaps in one message; a tool that says "I don't know the suburb" is better than one that guesses.
- **CTA:** "Save this and hand it to whoever answers your phone."

### 4. Notes in the ute, invoice never sent
- **Problem:** work is done, notes are scrawled, the invoice never happens.
- **Angle:** end-of-day notes to a draft invoice, with the rule that the model extracts and the code adds up.
- **Format:** 45-second video: real notes on screen, draft invoice out.
- **Three points:** why a line-itemed draft beats a blank; the rates table as the source of truth; arithmetic never goes to the model.
- **CTA:** "How many days between job done and invoice sent in your business? Be honest."

### 5. The spreadsheet that is actually your CRM
- **Problem:** the business runs on a spreadsheet, a shared inbox and a group chat, and is told it "needs a CRM".
- **Angle:** the spreadsheet is a CRM. Name what it lacks (history, ownership, reports) and pick the first one that is costing money.
- **Format:** text, contrarian.
- **Three points:** history (who changed what); ownership (whose job is that lead); reporting (the number you cannot produce today). Fix in that order.
- **CTA:** "Which of the three is hurting you most right now? That's your first project, not the whole platform."

### 6. Who holds the Salesforce login when your admin leaves?
- **Problem:** the only admin resigns and the credentials leave with them.
- **Angle:** interim custodianship as a one-page decision an owner can make today, from the SunRise engagement.
- **Format:** text with the custodianship template.
- **Three points:** name a custodian before you need one; a second admin login held by the owner, used never; what to revoke on the day someone leaves.
- **CTA:** "Write the custodian's name in the comments or in your own notes. Either counts."

### 7. You are paying for four licences and using two
- **Problem:** licence spend with nobody reviewing the table.
- **Angle:** the licence table in Company Information is the most valuable screen an owner can read without an admin.
- **Format:** text with a screenshot of the 4/4 and 0/6 table.
- **Three points:** how to read the table; Platform versus Salesforce licences in one sentence; the blank-login user as the tell.
- **CTA:** "Comment 'licence' for the query."

### 8. The lead rule that stopped matching your territory
- **Problem:** leads land with the wrong rep and nobody knows why a quarter was missed.
- **Angle:** rules do not update themselves. Every map change needs a rule audit.
- **Format:** text with a before-and-after sketch of two regions.
- **Three points:** how to find every assignment rule; the audit question ("does this still describe the map?"); reversibility, so fixing it is safe.
- **CTA:** "When did your territories last change? Then when did your rules?"

### 9. Three questions before buying any AI for your business
- **Problem:** AI bought on a board directive, licensed, never switched on.
- **Angle:** the Meridian pattern applied to any purchase: who owns it, what counts as success, what it must never say.
- **Format:** carousel, five slides.
- **Three points:** name an owner; define the countable outcome before go-live; write the list of answers the tool is never allowed to improvise.
- **CTA:** "If you've bought AI you haven't switched on, you're not alone. Comment 'three' and I'll send the one-page version."

### 10. Why your customer count changes depending on who you ask
- **Problem:** sales, service and accounts each have a different number.
- **Angle:** three systems, three definitions. The fix is a written definition of "customer", not a tool.
- **Format:** text.
- **Three points:** the three definitions in plain words; pick one and write it down; the identity-resolution rule as "which record wins".
- **CTA:** "Ask three people in your business how many customers you have. Post the spread."

### 11. The one thing an AI is allowed to change in your schedule
- **Problem:** owners fear an AI that "does things" in their systems.
- **Angle:** many read tools, one write path, every constraint listed. From the Jobs MCP server.
- **Format:** text with the constraint list as a screenshot.
- **Three points:** reading is safe; the single write is where the design work lives; the threat model is written down, not implied.
- **CTA:** "What's the one thing you'd let an AI change in your business? Just one."

### 12. Gas leak, live wire, water through the ceiling
- **Problem:** a booking tool that helpfully books an emergency for Tuesday.
- **Angle:** the escalation list as a product decision, and the transcript that shows it working.
- **Format:** 45-second video (doc 7) or text with the transcript.
- **Three points:** the three triggers; what the customer is told; the human is paged with the whole conversation attached.
- **CTA:** "Add your trade's emergency list in the comments. I'll extend the agent's."

### 13. The five-line Friday email
- **Problem:** projects drift because nobody writes down decisions.
- **Angle:** done, next, risks, decisions needed, metrics. To the sponsor, every Friday, no attachments.
- **Format:** text with the template.
- **Three points:** why five lines; why "decisions needed" is the line that matters; what two months of them gives you.
- **CTA:** "Copy it. Send it this Friday. Tell me if it survived."

### 14. Reversible or irreversible: the only two piles
- **Problem:** owners cannot tell which of a consultant's recommendations are safe to approve quickly.
- **Angle:** every change gets one of two labels. Reversible ones can be approved in a minute; irreversible ones wait.
- **Format:** text with the eight-decision table screenshot.
- **Three points:** what makes a change irreversible in Salesforce (merges, deletions, field type changes); why a consultant should refuse to touch the second pile without a written yes; how it speeds up the first pile.
- **CTA:** "Ask your next consultant which pile each recommendation is in."

### 15. Check where the wrong number was quoted before you fix it
- **Problem:** a corrected number becomes a restatement if it has been promised to someone.
- **Angle:** the exposure check as a habit: search reports, dashboards, templates and files first.
- **Format:** text with the exposure table.
- **Three points:** cleanup versus restatement; the four places to search inside the org; the honest limit (outside the org is still an open question).
- **CTA:** "Save this for the next time a number turns out to be wrong."

### 16. Why 28% of your customers call twice
- **Problem:** repeat contact, and the cost of a customer repeating themselves.
- **Angle:** from the Meridian vitals: 28% repeat contact rate, 9:40 handle time. The handoff that carries the transcript is the fix, human or AI.
- **Format:** text.
- **Three points:** the repeat-contact rate as the number to watch; why customers call back (no answer, no record, no owner); the transcript-carrying handoff rule.
- **CTA:** "Do you know your repeat contact rate? If not, that's this week's number to find."

### 17. A merge that overwrites the right phone number loses a customer
- **Problem:** duplicate cleanup done carelessly destroys data with no undo.
- **Angle:** Salesforce keeps no history of overwritten survivor fields. Log every merge before you make it.
- **Format:** text with the merge log screenshot.
- **Three points:** what a merge overwrites; the log format (survivor, absorbed, date); the seven groups with no log and what that cost.
- **CTA:** "If you've merged records without a log, you're in good company. Start the log today."

### 18. The AI is not allowed to add up your invoice
- **Problem:** owners assume AI is good at numbers.
- **Angle:** models are unreliable at arithmetic; the design keeps sums in code.
- **Format:** text, short, with one worked example.
- **Three points:** extraction is the model's job; the sum is the code's job; how the eval harness catches the difference.
- **CTA:** "Where else in your business is a tool doing maths nobody checks?"

### 19. What "first response in 48 hours" costs when it is regulated
- **Problem:** SLAs treated as dashboard decoration.
- **Angle:** the Kurrajong pattern: an energy retailer where a missed milestone means an ombudsman letter. Find the cost of a miss before building the clock.
- **Format:** text.
- **Three points:** three SLA tiers and why they differ; the milestone as a clock with a consequence; what to ask before configuring anything.
- **CTA:** "What happens in your business when a response deadline is missed? Nothing is an answer, and a useful one."

### 20. What to demand from any consultant who leaves
- **Problem:** the handover that is a folder of screenshots and nothing else.
- **Angle:** the deliverables folder as the standard: build log, SOPs, decision list, custodianship, the queries.
- **Format:** carousel, seven slides, one artefact per slide.
- **Three points:** the build log (every change, dated, with the reason); the SOPs (what to do when a rule breaks, how to deactivate a user); the decision list with what is still open.
- **CTA:** "Before your next engagement ends, send this to the consultant. Mine is public; hold me to it."
