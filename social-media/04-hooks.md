# 4. Thirty hooks

**Post topic:** your CRM's customer count is wrong, and how to find out by how much
**Source story:** a simulated solar installer whose dashboard said 301 customers. After merging 250 duplicate records, the answer was 41 households.
**Audience:** owners and operations managers of Australian trades and service businesses
**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Purpose:** build trust
**Limit:** every hook under 15 words

The post itself must carry the disclosure and the numbers behind each hook.
None of these promises anything the story cannot show.

---

## Curiosity

1. The dashboard said 301 customers. The true number was 41.
2. I found the real customer count in a field nobody looks at.
3. There is one report in your CRM that is quietly lying to you.
4. Why does the customer count change depending on who you ask?
5. Nobody had checked the number. It had been on the dashboard for a year.

## Problem-focused

6. Your CRM counts records. You wanted customers. Those are different numbers.
7. If a customer rings from a second phone, your CRM makes a second customer.
8. Every duplicate account is a customer your reps are contacting twice.
9. A wrong customer count breaks every number built on top of it.
10. You cannot forecast repeat work from a list where everyone appears seven times.

## Contrarian

11. Don't dedupe your CRM yet. Find out where the wrong number has been quoted.
12. The duplicates are not the problem. The merge rule you approved is.
13. A bigger customer list is not good news. Check it before you celebrate.
14. Your CRM does not need cleaning. It needs a definition of "customer".
15. Merging duplicates can lose data. Salesforce keeps no history of overwritten fields.

## Mistake-based

16. The merge rule matched on phone number. 127 records had no phone number.
17. We picked the survivor by "most recent won deal". Half the pairs had none.
18. The first mistake: correcting the number before checking who had quoted it.
19. I reconstructed seven merges after the fact. There was no log. My mistake.
20. Answering "how many customers" with a record count is the most common CRM error.

## Result-focused

21. 50 merge groups. 250 records absorbed. 51 accounts left. One honest number.
22. After the cleanup, the business could say how many customers it had. First time.
23. One query, thirty minutes, and the customer count stopped being a guess.
24. The corrected count was a cleanup, not a restatement. Here is the proof.
25. From 301 to 41 in three days, with every merge logged.

## Story-based

26. The owner asked how many customers he had. I said "it depends".
27. Day two in a solar company's org. Amelia Martin existed eight times.
28. I approved a merge rule with the client at 10am. It broke by 11.
29. The report was right. The question was wrong. Here is the difference.
30. Six updates sent, none read. The seventh was a table. Answered within the hour.
