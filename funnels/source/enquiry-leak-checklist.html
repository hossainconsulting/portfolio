The Enquiry-Leak Checklist

For trade and home-service owners. Tick what is true today. Every blank box is a place an enquiry can leak.

A. First reply

Every enquiry (call, text, form, DM) lands in one place I check.
Someone replies within 15 minutes in business hours, even if only to say when I will call.
After hours, the customer gets an automatic reply that sets expectations.

B. Quote

I collect the same basic details every time (address, job type, photos, urgency).
A quote goes out within one business day of the site visit or call.
Quotes are stored where I can find them by customer name in under a minute.

C. Follow-up

One named person owns every open quote.
An unanswered quote gets a follow-up at day 2 and again at day 7.
I know how many quotes I sent last month and how many turned into jobs.

D. Handover

The technician gets address, access notes, scope and price in one message.
The customer is told the booking time and who is coming, the day before.
After the job, the invoice goes out within 48 hours and I ask for a review.
Score: ______ / 12.   10-12: tidy, look for small wins.   6-9: real leaks, fix the first blank box in section A or C.   0-5: you are probably losing jobs weekly.

What one leak costs you

Use your own numbers. Be conservative.

Enquiries per week 
Percent that get no reply or a slow reply (your best guess) 
Percent of those who would have booked 
Average job value ($) 
Lost per week = enquiries × slow % × would-book % × job value 
Lost per year = weekly × 48 

Example: 20 enquiries × 25% slow × 30% would book × $800 = $1,200 a week.

Three fixes to try this week (no software needed)

1. One inbox. Forward the web form, set a missed-call text-back on your mobile, and check one list twice a day.
2. Day 2 and day 7. Put two recurring reminders in your phone for every open quote. Use a two-line message: "Hi [name], any questions on the quote? Happy to adjust."
3. One handover text. Copy the same template for every job: address, access, scope, price, customer phone.
